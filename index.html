About

I'm an MD/PhD candidate in Bioengineering at the University of Pennsylvania, working on artificial intelligence for medicine and biology.

Most medical AI systems perform well on a benchmark, get published, and never reach the patients they were built for. My work is about the specific reasons why.

How a system enters practice.I proposed that clinical AI should take on responsibility gradually, the way a physician in training does, starting from a supervised role and calibrated at the hospital using it.

Whether a clinician can see the reasoning.In work on schizophrenia detection from brain MRI and vocal fold paralysis from voice recordings, we built explainability into the models rather than reporting accuracy alone.

Whether it works for patients it wasn't trained on.Radiograph interpretation models are trained almost entirely on adults. I built an internationally validated system for adapting them to newborns.

Whether it works on equipment it wasn't trained on.Segmentation models tend to degrade on scanners they weren't trained on, which limits where they can be used. I built a brain tissue segmentation architecture that holds up across unseen scanners and sites.

I work with Pranav Rajpurkar and Eric Topol at Harvard Medical School on medical imaging and radiology reporting, and with Katalin Susztak at Penn on single-cell foundation models. At Columbia I built brain MRI architectures with Jia Guo, and at Massachusetts Eye and Ear I worked with Phillip Song and Allen Feng on machine learning for voice disorders and on a surgical force sensor.

Before Penn I was a Schwarzman Scholar at Tsinghua University and studied biomedical engineering at Columbia.

Selected work

Nature2025

Multimodal Generative AI for Medical Image Interpretation

What it would take for generative models to read medical images the way clinicians do, and how such a system should be introduced into practice: supervised at first, calibrated locally, given autonomy only as it earns it.

Paper

Nature Biotech2026

Generalist Biological AI in Modeling the Language of Life

Where generalist models are delivering in biology, where they aren't, and what has to change for them to become usable scientific instruments.

Paper

Nature BMEIn press

Repurposing Adult AI Radiograph Models for Neonatal Care

Radiograph interpretation models are trained almost entirely on adults. Using continual learning, we adapted them to neonatal care and validated the approach across 30 NICUs in 19 countries, without losing performance on the population they came from.

Code

Front. Neuroimaging2022

Improving Across-Dataset Brain Tissue Segmentation Using Transformer

Segmentation models tend to fail on MRI from scanners and sites they were not trained on. This hybrid CNN and transformer architecture was designed and tested for exactly that case.

Paper/Code

Scientific Reports2023

Detecting Schizophrenia With 3D Structural Brain MRI Using Deep Learning

No psychiatrist will act on a black box. Alongside the classification models, we identified which brain regions each prediction depended on, so the output can be checked rather than trusted.

Paper

PLOS Digital Health2024

Identifying Bias in Models That Detect Vocal Fold Paralysis From Audio

Voice screening models can look accurate overall while failing particular patients. Using explainable machine learning alongside clinician ratings, we traced which acoustic features the models relied on and where that produced bias.

Paper

PSB2025

ReXErr: Synthesizing Clinically Meaningful Errors in Radiology Reports

Report generation models can only be tested against the mistakes that matter if those mistakes exist in labelled form. ReXErr generates them at scale, graded by clinical severity.
